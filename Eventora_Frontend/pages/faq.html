Help Centre

Frequently asked questions.

How booking works, what vendors are charged, how the AI actually decides — and what is built versus still on the way.

Eventora is an AI-powered event management platform. It connects people planning an event with local vendors — event planners, caterers, decorators, photographers, DJs, mehndi artists and venues — and handles the whole journey from search through booking, payment and review in one place.

It is built for every kind of event, not just weddings: birthdays, engagements, corporate events, conferences, seminars, family gatherings and cultural events are all in scope.

Not yet. Eventora is a Final Year Project under active development in the Department of Information Technology at the University of Sindh, Jamshoro. A pilot in Hyderabad and Jamshoro is planned once the platform is complete.

Anything on this site that looks like vendor listings, ratings or client feedback is clearly labelled as demo or sample content. We do not present placeholder data as real.

We start with Hyderabad and Jamshoro, then expand across Sindh — Sukkur, Larkana and Mirpurkhas — before going nationwide.

That order is deliberate. Existing platforms concentrate on Karachi and Lahore, which leaves secondary cities without a serious digital option. Those cities are our starting point, not an afterthought.

Vendors are organised into seven categories: Event Planner, Catering, Decoration, Photography, DJ, Mehndi and Venue.

Browsing vendors and creating a client account are free. You pay the vendor's price for whatever you book — nothing extra is added to your bill.

Eventora earns a commission from the vendor on completed bookings. See the Payments section for exactly how that is calculated.

Create an account and choose the Client role, then create your event with its type, date, city, guest count and budget. From there you can browse vendors yourself or ask for AI recommendations scored against that event.

Eventora uses an event-first model: every booking belongs to an event. This is a deliberate architectural decision, not an extra hurdle.

A wedding usually means five or six separate vendors. Grouping them under one event keeps them as a single coherent plan — one budget, one date, one guest count — instead of six unrelated transactions. It is also what lets the recommendation engine understand your requirements.

You do not have to create the event first, though. If you click Book on a vendor before you have one, we ask you to pick an existing event or create one right there, then take you back to where you left off.

The full sequence is:

  • You select or create the event the booking belongs to
  • You choose a specific service from a vendor
  • You review the details and confirm — the request is sent as pending
  • The vendor accepts or rejects it
  • Once accepted, payment is unlocked
  • After the service is delivered the booking is marked completed
  • You can then leave a rating and review

Nothing is saved as a booking until you confirm at the review step, so an abandoned booking never leaves a half-finished record behind.

  • Pending — request sent, the vendor has not responded yet
  • Accepted — the vendor has approved it and payment is now possible
  • Rejected — the vendor declined the request
  • Completed — the service has been delivered; you can now leave a review
  • Cancelled — the booking was called off before delivery

Rejected and cancelled are kept separate on purpose: one is a vendor declining, the other is a booking being called off. Collapsing them into a single status would hide which actually happened.

The booking is marked rejected and you are notified. Your event and any other bookings under it are unaffected — you simply choose another vendor for that part of your plan.

The commission is a percentage of the booking total, charged to the vendor. On a booking of Rs. 100,000 at the current 10% rate:

  • You pay Rs. 100,000
  • Eventora receives Rs. 10,000
  • The vendor receives Rs. 90,000

The percentage used is stored with each booking, so historical records stay accurate even if the default rate changes later.

A Python recommendation engine built with Scikit-learn scores available services against the event you have created. It weighs your event type, date, city, guest count and budget against each service's price, capacity and category, along with the vendor's rating and the sentiment of their reviews.

It scores individual services rather than businesses, because one vendor may offer several packages at very different prices — but results are grouped and presented by vendor so they are easy to read.
